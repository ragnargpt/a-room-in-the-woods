# The woodland style

Keep this visual language consistent as a room grows into a landscape. The aim is a quiet, inhabited place with convincing scale and an illustrated finish.

## Shape and space

- Model a real three-dimensional space, not a flat backdrop. Room proportions come from photographs and measurements. Hidden sides and uncertain dimensions must be identified as inferred.
- Use readable silhouettes, modest bevels and simplified forms. A chair still needs its characteristic back, arms, seat and base. A doorway needs thickness, reveals and hardware.
- Use metres throughout. Doors, steps, seats, tree trunks and dock boards should agree with a person's scale.
- Vary tree height, density, age and spacing. Leave clearings and viewpoints. Keep multiple layers of trees, shore and distant hills visible.
- Build plausible connections: room → entrance → stairs → path → clearing → shore → dock. Keep grass and rocks out of circulation routes and water.

## Color and materials

| Role | Palette direction |
|---|---|
| Pines and shaded foliage | Deep blue-green, sage, muted moss |
| Timber and close ground | Warm oak, weathered honey, restrained earth |
| Structure | Rough charcoal metal, subtle wear, no glossy black plastic |
| Interiors | Mineral whites, warm wood, recognizable personal accents |
| Day | Soft blue-green distance and a pale warm horizon |
| Sunset | Broad peach and coral sky, red/purple distant layers, warm edge light |
| Night | Deep blue, readable silhouettes, small local warm lights |

Textures should explain the material rather than create visual noise. Wood grain has a believable scale and direction. Concrete is rough and uneven without becoming a high-contrast camouflage pattern. Use geometry for visible thickness; do not try to paint important structural form into a flat image.

## Light and life

- Compute the sun and moon from the chosen place and actual date/time. Use those same directions for the visible bodies and directional lights.
- The warm sunset palette is an artistic treatment around the real sun's elevation. It is not a weather forecast or exact atmospheric simulation.
- Let lake ripples travel and break up reflections. Keep a readable dark depth, shallower shoreline, broad sky reflection and small highlights. A lake should not look like a blue floor or a violent sea.
- Keep wind, fire, boat motion and sound slow and sparse. All can be quieted. Stillness is part of the result.
- No mandatory characters. A room can feel lived in through a lamp, cup, book, chair and the user's own small details.

## Acceptance

Compare the same view in daylight, sunset and night. Inspect at least one room close-up, one exterior, one ground-level path and one shore-level lake view. Watch several seconds of movement. Fix wrong proportions and intersections before decorative effects. Preserve this style when adding an object; do not introduce unrelated photoreal assets, exaggerated cartoon figures, dense black outlines or neon UI into the scene.
